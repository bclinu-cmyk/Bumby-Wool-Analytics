# Bumby Wool KPI Framework & Analytics

## MGMT 6219 Data Analytics Capstone | Riipen Industry Project

### About the Project

This project was completed as part of the MGMT 6219 Data Analytics Capstone in collaboration with Bumby Wool, a Canadian-made sustainable fashion brand.

The main business challenge was data fragmentation. Bumby Wool had information across Shopify, Meta/Facebook and Instagram, and Google Analytics, but there was no centralized process for turning the information into a consistent set of business insights and KPIs.

Our team developed a practical KPI framework and analytics solution designed to help a small business make faster, data-informed decisions.

The available Shopify data covered April 1 to July 13, 2026. We cleaned and analyzed the available data, developed KPIs, created dashboard concepts and recommendations, and communicated the findings through a professional presentation.

The project originally aimed to connect Shopify, Meta, and Google Analytics data into a unified reporting framework. However, Meta and GA4 data were not available in the final export, so these areas were identified as future opportunities rather than presenting unsupported results.

## Project Highlights

### Customer Retention

One of the most important findings was the low repeat-buyer rate.

Only **18 of 128 customers** made a repeat purchase, resulting in a repeat-buyer rate of approximately **14%**. This identified customer retention as one of the strongest opportunities for Bumby Wool.

The team recommended developing a post-purchase email flow and working toward increasing repeat purchases toward 20%.

### Revenue Performance

The analysis identified approximately **$27,618 in total revenue** from 151 valid revenue orders after excluding 14 test orders.

Revenue increased through the spring months, although July was treated carefully because it represented only a partial month and included one unusually large $5,963 bulk order.

### Order Value

The median order value was **$101**, compared with a mean of **$183**.

The median was considered a more useful representation of a typical order because several large orders pulled the average upward.

### Product Performance

Among matched catalog items, **Tops** generated approximately $5,016 and **Diaper Covers** generated approximately $3,951, making them the strongest product categories identified in the analysis.

However, only approximately 54% of line items matched the available product catalog, so these results were treated as directional.

### U.S. Market

Approximately **75% of orders shipped to the United States**, compared with 21% to Canada.

This finding suggested that the U.S. should be treated as an important market when considering shipping, pricing, tax handling, and future marketing strategy.

## Analytical Approach

The project followed a five-stage process:

1. **Clean & Govern** – removed personally identifiable information, identified test orders, and reviewed data quality.
2. **Explore** – examined revenue, order values, products, and customer behavior.
3. **Frame KPIs** – developed a KPI framework covering sales, product, customer, and marketing performance.
4. **Build Dashboard** – developed and tested an interactive Power BI dashboard.
5. **Recommend** – translated the findings into practical business recommendations.

The project used Excel and Python for data preparation and analysis and Power BI/DAX for dashboarding and visualization.

---

## Key Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the team recommended:

- Develop a post-purchase email strategy to increase repeat purchases.
- Work toward improving the repeat-buyer rate from approximately 14% toward 20%.
- Focus inventory, placement, and bundles on strong categories such as Tops and Diaper Covers.
- Treat the United States as a primary market.
- Continue discounting while improving campaign tracking.
- Improve product naming and SKU consistency.
- Connect Meta and GA4 data to the reporting model.
- Use the dashboard as a weekly management tool.
- Revisit seasonality after a full year of clean data is available.

---

## Limitations

The project was based on approximately 3.5 months of available Shopify data, so strong seasonality conclusions could not be made.

Approximately 46% of line items did not match the available product catalog, limiting the reliability of product-level analysis.

Meta advertising and GA4 website data were also unavailable in the final export. Therefore, metrics such as ROAS, CPA, and website funnel performance were included in the KPI framework as future metrics but were not presented as calculated findings.

Recognizing these limitations was an important part of maintaining responsible and evidence-based analytics.
